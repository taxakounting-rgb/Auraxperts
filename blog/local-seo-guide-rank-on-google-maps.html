Local SEO: A Practical Guide to Ranking on Google Maps

AuraXperts Team 25 Feb 2025 4 min read
Share

When someone searches for a service 'near me', the businesses in the map results win most of the calls. Here's how local SEO works and how to get your business into that list.

Think about the last time you searched for a restaurant, a clinic or a repair shop. Chances are you didn't scroll through ten pages of results. You looked at the small map at the top of Google, compared a few businesses by their star ratings and photos, and called one of them. That map section — often called the "local pack" — is where a huge share of local business is won or lost.

Local SEO is the practice of improving your visibility in those results. The good news is that it's one of the most accessible forms of marketing for small businesses. You don't need a huge budget; you need accuracy, consistency and patience.

How Google decides who appears in the map

Google has said publicly that local rankings are based mainly on three factors: relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance is how well your business profile matches what the person searched for. Distance is how far you are from the searcher or the location they mentioned. Prominence is how well-known and trusted your business appears to be, based on things like reviews, links and mentions across the web.

You can't change where your shop is located, but you have a lot of control over relevance and prominence. That's where your effort should go.

Start with your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is the listing that appears in Maps and in the local pack. If you haven't claimed and verified it, that's your first job. Once verified, fill in every section as completely as possible. Choose the most accurate primary category — this is one of the strongest relevance signals — and add secondary categories for other services you genuinely offer.

Write a clear description of what you do and the areas you serve. Add your opening hours and keep them updated, especially around public holidays. Upload real photos of your premises, your team and your work; profiles with good photos tend to receive more clicks and direction requests. Use the "Products" and "Services" sections to list what you sell, and post updates regularly — an offer, a recent project or a helpful tip. An active profile signals to both Google and customers that you're open for business.

Reviews: the engine of local trust

Reviews influence local rankings, but more importantly, they influence people. A business with forty recent reviews averaging 4.7 stars will usually get the call over one with three reviews from two years ago, even if it's slightly further away.

The simplest way to get more reviews is to ask. After a successful job, send your customer a short, friendly message with a direct link to your review form. Make it easy; most people are happy to help if it takes less than a minute. Then reply to every review — thank the positive ones personally, and respond calmly and helpfully to negative ones. Future customers read those replies, and a thoughtful response to criticism can build more trust than a perfect score.

Keep your business details consistent everywhere

Your business name, address and phone number should be written exactly the same way on your website, your Google profile, your Facebook page, and any directories you're listed in. Inconsistencies — an old phone number here, a slightly different business name there — create confusion for customers and make it harder for Google to be confident about your details.

Make your website support your local presence

Your website and your Google profile work together. Make sure your site clearly mentions the city or areas you serve, includes your full contact details, and has a dedicated page for each main service. If you serve several cities, consider creating a useful page for each one — not a copy-pasted template with the city name swapped, but genuinely helpful information for customers in that area.

Your site also needs to load quickly and work perfectly on mobile, because that's where most local searches happen. Embedding a Google Map on your contact page and adding local business structured data can help search engines understand your location.

Earn local mentions and links

Prominence grows when other credible sources mention you. Local news sites, community organisations, industry associations, event sponsorships and partnerships with complementary businesses are all opportunities. A local supplier listing you as a partner, or a news story about a community project you supported, sends a stronger signal than dozens of low-quality directory listings.

Be patient and track progress

Local SEO compounds over time. You may see small improvements within weeks, but meaningful results usually take a few months of consistent effort. Track the calls, website visits and direction requests reported in your Google Business Profile, and monitor how you rank for your most important searches.

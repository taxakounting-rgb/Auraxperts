How to Plan a Month of Social Media Content in One Afternoon

AuraXperts Team 8 Apr 2025 4 min read
Share

Posting consistently is the hardest part of social media for most businesses. Batch planning solves it — here's a simple system you can use every month.

Most businesses know they should be posting regularly on social media. The problem isn't motivation; it's time. Coming up with a fresh idea every morning, designing a graphic, writing a caption and posting it — while also running the business — is exhausting. So posting becomes irregular: three posts one week, nothing for the next three, and the audience slowly forgets you exist.

The solution is batching. Instead of deciding what to post every day, you plan, create and schedule a whole month in one focused session. Here's the process we use with clients, adapted so you can do it yourself.

Step one: decide what you want social media to achieve

Before thinking about posts, be clear about the goal. Do you want more WhatsApp enquiries? More people visiting your shop? More bookings? Brand awareness in a new city? Your goal shapes everything else. A clinic aiming for appointment bookings will create very different content from a clothing brand launching a new collection.

Write the goal down in one sentence. When you're unsure whether a post idea is worthwhile, check it against that sentence.

Step two: choose four content pillars

Content pillars are recurring themes that keep your feed varied but focused. Four works well for most businesses. A typical set might be: educate (helpful tips that show your expertise), showcase (your products, projects or results), behind the scenes (your team, your process, your workspace), and promote (offers, launches and direct calls to action).

Pillars solve the blank-page problem. Instead of asking "What should I post?", you ask "What's a good educate post this week?" — a much easier question. They also keep you from posting nothing but sales messages, which audiences quickly tune out.

Step three: pick a realistic posting rhythm

Consistency matters far more than volume. Three good posts a week, every week, will outperform a burst of daily posting followed by silence. Look honestly at the time and resources you have, and choose a rhythm you can sustain for months, not days. You can always increase it later.

Step four: brainstorm ideas in bulk

Now set a timer for thirty minutes and generate ideas for each pillar. Good sources include the questions customers ask you every week, objections you hear before people buy, your best-selling products, recent projects you're proud of, and upcoming events or seasons. Aim for at least five ideas per pillar. Don't judge them yet — just write.

Once you have a list, pick the strongest ideas and note the format that suits each one. A step-by-step tip often works well as a carousel; a transformation or process works well as a short reel; an announcement might be a single striking image.

Step five: map everything onto a calendar

Open a simple spreadsheet or calendar and assign each idea to a date, rotating through your pillars so no two similar posts sit side by side. Mark important dates first — sales, holidays, product launches, events — and build around them. Leave one or two empty slots each week for timely content, such as a trending topic or a recent client success.

Step six: design and write in batches

This is where batching saves the most time. Create a handful of branded templates using your brand colours and fonts, then design all the month's graphics in one sitting. Next, write all the captions together. Each caption should open with a line that makes people stop scrolling, deliver the value of the post clearly, and end with a single call to action — comment, save, share, visit the website or send a message.

Working this way keeps your visual style consistent and lets you get into a creative rhythm instead of constantly switching tasks.

Step seven: schedule, engage and review

Use Meta Business Suite or another scheduling tool to queue up the month's posts. Scheduling doesn't mean disappearing, though: set aside a few minutes each day to reply to comments and messages, because engagement is where relationships — and sales — actually happen.

At the end of the month, review your results. Which posts reached the most people? Which ones generated messages or website visits? Do more of what worked, drop what didn't, and use those lessons to plan next month even faster.
